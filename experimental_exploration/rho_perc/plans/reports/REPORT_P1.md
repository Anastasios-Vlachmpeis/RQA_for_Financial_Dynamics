# REPORT P1 — triviality audit

**What was run.** 13 processes × 2 transforms (`z`, `rank`) × 2 Theiler settings (Article 4 rule; minimum $(m-1)\tau+1$) × 50 reps, $N=400$ states, $m=3$, $\delta=0.05$, 8-neighbourhood, using `rhoperc.prep.theiler_rule`/`distance` and `rhoperc.processes.generate` via `src/rhoperc/experiments/p1.py`. Grid and definitions are frozen in `notebooks/tables/rho_perc/P1/spec.json` (written before the results; it records the frozen `core.py` SHA-256 `3ad7c180…c636`, matching `plans/rho_perc/FROZEN.json`). Per run, `runs.csv` (2,600 rows) saves $\varepsilon$ and $\rho$ for $\rho_{\rm perc}$ and both bounds, `too_persistent`, the Theiler $w$ used, and the seed `[1, cell, rep]`; `by_process.csv` has 52 rows; the figure is `notebooks/tables/rho_perc/P1/figures/rho_bounds_by_process.png` ($\rho_{\rm iso}$, $\rho_{\rm perc}$, $\rho_{\rm lag}$ per process, $z$ + Theiler rule). Full run took 16.5 s of compute (`run.log` in the same folder, which also records the rerun of `python -m rhoperc.selfcheck`: K1–K9 ALL PASS, exit 0). Position $\pi=(\rho_{\rm perc}-\rho_{\rm iso})/(\rho_{\rm lag}-\rho_{\rm iso})$; the guard for $\rho_{\rm lag}=\rho_{\rm iso}$ sets $\pi$ to NaN (defined in `spec.json`), and that case occurred in 0 of 2,600 runs (`runs.csv`).

**Gate G1: PASS.** Computed by code in `notebooks/tables/rho_perc/P1/decision.csv`: 11 of 11 in-scope processes are free under the Theiler rule + `z` transform (rule: at least 6 of 11 free). X1 and X2 were run and reported but never counted, as required.

**Classification of every process** (Theiler rule + `z`, from `by_process.csv`; share pinned = pinned-high + pinned-low share over 50 runs): all free — S1 (0.00 pinned), S2 (0.00), S3 (0.18, all pinned low), S4 (0.02, pinned low), S5 (0.00), S6 (0.08, pinned low), D1 (0.00), D2 (0.00), D3 (0.00), F1 (0.08; 0.04 high, 0.04 low), F2 (0.00). No in-scope process is pinned high or pinned low as a class. Out of scope: X1 free (0.38 pinned; 0.22 high, 0.16 low), X2 free by the same <½ rule (0.46 pinned, all pinned high) — reported only, never counted.

**Findings** (all from `notebooks/tables/rho_perc/P1/by_process.csv` unless stated):

1. Pinning is rare in scope: the largest pinned share under the rule + `z` is S3 at 0.18 (all pinned low), then S6 and F1 at 0.08; the other eight in-scope processes are at 0.00–0.02. Across all 2,600 runs in `runs.csv`, $\rho_{\rm iso} \le \rho_{\rm perc} \le \rho_{\rm lag}$ held with 0 violations.
2. Median $\pi$ for the 11 in-scope processes (rule + `z`) ranges from 0.296 (S3) to 0.753 (F1), so $\rho_{\rm perc}$ typically sits strictly between its bounds rather than on either one; F1 is closest to the upper bound.
3. Not being pinned is not the same as being uncorrelated with a bound: F1 has Spearman($\rho_{\rm perc}$, $\rho_{\rm lag}$) = 0.929 (rule + `z`) despite only 0.08 of runs pinned, and across in-scope processes Spearman with $\rho_{\rm iso}$ ranges from −0.489 (D1) to 0.790 (S5) and with $\rho_{\rm lag}$ from −0.146 (S6) to 0.929 (F1).
4. The `too_persistent` flag is almost only an out-of-scope phenomenon: share 0.00 for all in-scope processes except S3 (0.02), against 0.66 for X1 and 0.98 for X2 (`runs.csv`).

**What the Theiler rule changed (one sentence).** Compared with the minimum window, the Article 4 rule raised $w$ in only 14.2 % of (process, transform, rep) cells (mainly S3, 98 % of reps, and S6, 40 %), changed the pinned share by at most 0.14 (S3, `z`: pinned-low share 0.04 → 0.18), and changed no process's free/pinned classification (`by_process.csv`, `runs.csv`).

**Deviations.** None; no entry added to `plans/rho_perc/DEVIATIONS.md`. No STOP file was written.

**What it means for the paper.** $\rho_{\rm perc}$ is not merely a copy of $\rho_{\rm iso}$ or $\rho_{\rm lag}$ on any of the 11 in-scope process classes: under the standard settings it is pinned to a bound in at most 18 % of runs and usually lies strictly between them. The triviality objection is therefore answered for these classes, and the paper may proceed to ask (P2–P3) whether the extra information is real signal rather than noise.
